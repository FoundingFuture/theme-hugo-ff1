The escapement is the part of a clock that everyone looks at and nobody
explains. It has one job, which is to let the gear train advance by exactly
one tooth and no more, and to do it in a way that neither drains the
pendulum nor pushes it around.

Almost every mechanism worth reading about has this shape. Something has to
happen at a rate, and the thing that sets the rate must not be disturbed by
the thing it is regulating.

{{< pull >}}The regulator must not be disturbed by what it regulates.{{< /pull >}}

#### The same shape, elsewhere

Bicycles have it too, in the freewheel. So does the printing press, in the
mechanism that holds a sheet still while the platen comes down and then
lets it go before the ink has time to think about it.

These notes are about the second kind of cleverness, the sort that stops
being visible once it works.
