---
title: Escapements
date: 2026-08-01
description: The part that lets the train advance one tooth at a time.
---

An escapement does two things that pull against each other. It stops the
gear train, and it gives the pendulum a push to replace what friction took.
Every design is a different opinion about how to do the second without
spoiling the first.

## What it has to do

Hold the train, so the weight does not simply run down. Release it by
exactly one tooth, so the hands advance by a known amount. Return a little
energy to the pendulum, so it does not stop. Do all of that without
altering the rate, which is the part nobody manages perfectly.

## Where the designs differ

They differ in when the push arrives. A push delivered as the pendulum
passes through the bottom of its swing costs the least accuracy, because
that is where the pendulum is least sensitive to being disturbed. Every
escapement is an attempt to arrange the push nearer to that moment.

### Recoil

The wheel is pushed backwards for part of the swing. Cheap to make and
forgiving of poor workmanship, and it interferes throughout.

### Detached

The wheel and the pendulum touch only at the moment of release. Harder to
make, and the pendulum spends most of its swing free.

## Why it stopped mattering

A quartz oscillator has no escapement at all, and keeps better time than
the best of these by four orders of magnitude. What survives is the
problem, which turns up again wherever a rate has to be held.
